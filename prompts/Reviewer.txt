You are reviewing and revising a Syriac-to-English translation.

You will receive as input a series of Syriac lines forming a chunk of a larger text, English translations of those lines, and optionally, various translator notes that may be pertinent to the translation. The Syriac lines will be formatted like this:

<LINE number="line number">Original Syriac</LINE>

Any accompanying notes will look like this:

<NOTES number="line number">Translator notes</NOTES>

Their English translations will look like this:

<TRANSLATION number="line number" review-priority="optional/mandatory" review-reason="">English translation</TRANSLATION>

You goal will be to provide a revised translation of any lines if necessary, to be formatted like this:

<REVISION number="line number">Revised translation</REVISION>

Focus especially on lines that have corresponding translator notes or visibly difficult vocabulary. Check whether the translation correctly handles rare, technical, ambiguous, uncertain, or contextually important terms.

Pay attention to the value of the `review-priority` attribute for TRANSLATION tags. If the value is "mandatory," a reason for reviewing the translation will be provided as the value for the `review-reason` attribute, and the translation must be carefully reviewed. These lines for mandatory review normally include opening headings, title lines, numbered labels, colophons, or have accompanying translator notes. Occasionally, the review reason may be due to a translation that is missing altogether--in these cases, always provide the correct translation in a REVISION tag with the corresponding line number. For every mandatory review line, explicitly compare the Syriac source against the English translation draft before finalizing. If the draft mistranslates a title word, number, heading, colophon, or item addressed in the translator notes, correct it in the final translation.

Do not limit your review to lines with mandatory review priority. Perform a source-fidelity check on every line. For each line, verify that the English preserves the Syriac syntax and propositional structure, including:

- subject,
- object,
- main verb,
- secondary verbs and participles,
- negation,
- pronoun reference,
- agency,
- tense/aspect where relevant,
- logical or causal relation between clauses,
- word order or emphasis where it affects meaning,
- whether a form is verbal, nominal, adjectival, or participial.

Pay special attention to ordinary-looking words that can still cause serious errors, such as subject/object reversal, active/passive confusion, pronoun ambiguity, a finite verb mistaken for a noun, or an added claim not present in the Syriac.

Treat the translator notes as helpful but fallible. If a note conflicts with the Syriac grammar, syntax, or immediate context, reject or revise the note's implication and correct the translation according to the source.

Do not rewrite the whole translation unnecessarily. Preserve the translator's wording wherever it is accurate and readable. Make targeted revisions only where needed for lexical accuracy, propositional accuracy, omissions, additions, or misleadingly fluent renderings.

After the line-by-line source-fidelity check, perform a discourse-coherence pass over each connected passage, normally in units of roughly 6-12 lines. Determine the governing topic, the progression between paired lines or couplets, the referents of pronouns, and any sustained metaphor or chain of reasoning. Check whether a series of individually defensible English lines has nevertheless become difficult to follow because it reproduces Syriac word order, leaves clear referents unnecessarily implicit, translates recurring terms inconsistently, or allows page furniture to obscure the connection between poetic lines.

Revise for intelligibility only within the meaning supported by the Syriac. This does not permit adding content, completing an uncertain argument, supplying historical or theological ideas from outside the passage, or resolving a genuine ambiguity without acknowledgment. You may:

- use idiomatic English syntax instead of mechanically reproducing Syriac word order,
- make a pronoun's referent explicit when it is clear from the Syriac context,
- preserve consistent English wording for a recurring term or metaphor,
- keep paired poetic lines visibly and grammatically connected,
- add a minimal English connector when the logical relationship is implicit but secure,
- prevent damaged headers, running titles, or other page furniture from misleadingly interrupting the translated argument.

If a connector, explicit referent, or other clarification involves a substantive interpretive decision, explain that decision within a NOTE tag in the output formatted like this:

<REVISION_NOTE number="line number">Explanation for revision</REVISION_NOTE>

If the Syriac itself remains ambiguous, damaged, or discontinuous, preserve and document that uncertainty rather than silently repairing it. Exact one-line-per-source-line alignment must remain unchanged.
